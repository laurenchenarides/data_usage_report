Background

  • Food and agriculture researchers face growing obstacles to using data:
    • Limited and/or time-consuming discoverability of datasets
    • Barriers to access (e.g. cost, application processes, restricted use)
    • Lack of standards for citation, metadata, and linkage

Background

  • Meanwhile, demand for interoperable data-driven research is rising:
    • Policymakers and funders want more evidence-based insights
    • Reviewers and editors are being asked to review submissions with more complex data
    • Early-career researchers struggle to know where or how to start

The Initiative

This project responds by building tools that:

  1. surface food + ag data (search and discovery)
  2. connect researchers (citability)
  3. contextualize food + ag data (metadata standardization)

Does This Infrastructure Already Exist?

In short, no.

The components are there, but none are tailored specifically for food + ag.

Data Commons (Search for and Discover Data) Dryad (Generate Dataset DOIs) Croissant (Metadata Standardization)


What We’re Building

  • Metadata & research navigation layers
  • Linked dashboards (e.g., USDA FAR Dashboard, Digital Science Data Explorer, National Data Platform)
  • Researcher-facing tools to:
    • Discover data sources
    • Find collaborators (and referees!)
    • See who funds what
    • More…

Why Food & Ag Marketing Policy Section (FAMPS)?

  • FAMPS researchers often rely on proprietary, restricted, or siloed data
  • Projects increasingly integrate sources like scanner data, retail promotions, and household behavior
  • It’s not always clear what’s available, who funds it, or how it’s being used

This work supports:

  • Discoverability of marketing-relevant datasets
  • Visibility into underused or emerging sources (e.g., loyalty card data, branded content, spatial pricing)
  • Opportunities to surface funding patterns and potential collaborators

What Data Are Researchers Actually Using?

We reviewed thousands of food security publications to identify dataset usage.

Finding:

11 commonly used food and agriculture datasets account for only 2% of all research published on food security.

Read more here and here.

So what is being used? We’re cataloging and analyzing those patterns to inform future infrastructure needs.

  • Most food security research is not using USDA data
  • Many are drawing on novel, proprietary, or administrative sources
  • Citation practices (or lack thereof) make discoverability and standardization very difficult

Our Vision

To build a trusted, transparent, and researcher-informed data infrastructure that supports food and agricultural research.

  • Centered on usability, not just access
  • Built with — not just for — the research community
  • Designed to help different user communities (researchers, reviewers, and policymakers) make better decisions

A future where food and ag researchers can:

  • Find the right data — quickly
  • Understand how it’s been used
  • Trust the infrastructure behind it
  • Build on each other’s work — not start from scratch — and get attribution
